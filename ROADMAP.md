# Home energy and smart-charging project roadmap

**Status snapshot:** 2026-09-20

**Purpose:** bring local Growatt inverter/battery telemetry, Renault Zoe EV telemetry, Peblar charging, Zonneplan dynamic prices, and Home Assistant automation together for useful, safe smart charging and battery dispatch. The system should first observe and explain; automated actions follow only after live data, economics, constraints, and behavior have been validated.

## Update: Growatt TOU controls and broker follow-up (2026-09-20)

The Growatt TL-XH integration now exposes all nine inverter TOU periods as
Home Assistant controls in DEV: one priority selector, start/end times, and an
enable switch per period. Writes use Modbus FC10 for the complete two-register
period pair, preserve the other packed fields, and verify the result after a
controlled readback. A staging HIL test changed the end time of disabled period
2 temporarily and restored it without enabling the period. The global AC-charge
permission remains a separate H3049 control. The implementation and tests are
in the nested Growatt repository (`8ee0db8`); no production deployment has been
made.

The test also confirmed a broker responsibility that is still open: after a
successful register write, the broker can serve a stale cached block until its
normal cache refresh. The HA integration currently waits 11 seconds as a
temporary workaround. The permanent broker task is a serialized read-after-
write refresh of the affected complete block, with the actual device readback
committed atomically before the write response is considered complete. Once
that is implemented and tested for FC06 and FC10, remove the HA delay and keep
the broker as the single source of cache coherence.

## User experience target

The finished system should be approachable enough to hand to a nontechnical household member. They should be able to add it, understand what it is doing, and handle ordinary changes without editing YAML, reading logs, or knowing terms such as Modbus, CAN, MQTT, or registers. Use Home Assistant's standard setup and options flows for configuration; keep advanced diagnostics available when needed, and explain technical details in plain language at the point where they matter.

Price history is part of the normal user experience. Provide an easy-to-read chart with selectable time ranges, actual historical prices distinct from forecasts, and selectable averages/trend lines such as moving averages. Label units and whether a price includes taxes and other charges so users can compare like with like.

The system should also show how Zoe is expected to charge tomorrow: a predicted SoC curve, likely charging periods, estimated finish time and energy, and a clear indication of uncertainty. Keep the charge forecast separate from the price/EMS plan so the user can see both the intended schedule and the expected vehicle response. Model the household's week explicitly: Monday–Thursday can use configured workday departure targets; Friday–Sunday normally have no fixed departure, but spontaneous trips such as shopping remain possible. Do not invent a weekend departure or treat its absence as permission to make the car unavailable. Battery plans should explain their expected net value after conversion losses and an explicit allowance for battery wear.

Design the Zoe data path for two distinct operating phases. During commissioning, a continuously pollable ELM may be used for bounded debug captures and charge-model calibration. After its supply is moved from the always-powered diagnostic port to the READY-switched accessory socket, treat ELM availability as brief arrival/departure measurement windows: capture and timestamp direct SoC and odometer readings, then expect the car to be unobservable while parked or charging. Estimate intermediate charging SoC from the last direct reading plus observed EVSE energy/power and a calibrated relationship between delivered energy and SoC. Label estimates with their age and uncertainty, and anchor or correct them at the next direct SoC reading. Use odometer readings to mark trip boundaries and provide distance context. Never silently turn an estimate into a measured SoC value.

Model the relationship between EVSE current and battery energy gain as a planning input. The existing PyCanZE charge logs were recorded with a granny charger, so they provide a useful Zoe charging-rate and wall-to-SoC-efficiency baseline, not Peblar-specific efficiency. Preserve charger identity and measured versus requested current in each session. Characterize Peblar separately using its measured AC energy/power and paired direct SoC snapshots, then estimate efficiency by current setting and SoC band with uncertainty. Include vehicle/charger standby use, voltage, and temperature where available. Let the planner account for current-dependent conversion losses when it selects a Peblar current limit; do not transfer granny-charger efficiency as a verified Peblar value.

The planner should present a time-based battery SoC target schedule that a household member can understand and adjust. A nightly planning run around 01:00 should use the next day's prices, interval solar forecast, expected household demand, battery SoC and reserve, and EV connection/charge goal. The schedule can set different battery targets for different times of day: for example, preserve enough energy to bridge the 06:00–10:00 price peak, then leave room to absorb forecast solar without exporting more than necessary, and reach a chosen evening target by 17:00. Keep a safe configured minimum reserve; “make room for solar” must not mean discharging below it. EV charging should be scheduled in low-cost periods while satisfying a configured minimum SoC and any departure target. Friday–Sunday must remain usable without a default departure time.

## System principles

- Use a normalized, timestamped provider contract with units, validity, and freshness for every input. Keep source IDs and provenance available for diagnostics.
- Stale or contradictory Growatt, EV, Peblar, P1, or price inputs must produce a visible unavailable/failsafe plan and must never be interpreted as a valid zero.
- Keep PyCanZE vehicle communication passive and read-only for polling. Do not add drive, charging, or ECU write commands to the EV telemetry path.
- Keep the RPi USB/RS485 Modbus bus single-master. Direct serial HA polling and broker polling must never overlap.
- Observe Peblar and battery control capabilities from the actual installed integration and config entry before assuming a service, range, or persistence behavior.
- Separate planning from control. The planner must remain pure and testable; Home Assistant actions belong in narrow provider adapters with explicit bounds and status feedback.
- Represent battery SoC goals as time-indexed user policy, separate from live SoC and device control. Treat Growatt TOU windows and their discharge-power limits as a control surface whose exact semantics, persistence, slot capacity, and read-back behavior must be verified before use. A plan can propose TOU settings in shadow mode before any adapter writes them.
- Respect site import/fuse limits, vehicle and charger limits, household load, battery reserve, and user target/departure settings. The existing EMS architecture gives the precedence and safety constraints; preserve them as explicit policy, not hidden automation side effects.
- Keep EMS planning in shadow mode until provider freshness, planner behavior, actuator semantics, recovery behavior, and operator override have passed separate review. Manual DEV hardware tests are a separate, deliberate action through the staging UI; they do not authorize production HA writes or automatic dispatch.
- Treat the Zoe day-ahead prediction as an estimate with explicit confidence and input quality. Keep predicted SoC/finish time visibly distinct from observed measurements and planned charging windows.
- Evaluate every proposed battery charge/discharge against a life-aware net-benefit estimate: tariff value after round-trip losses, minus an evidence-based battery-wear cost. Respect reserve and operating limits, and do not cycle the battery for a margin below the estimated wear cost.
- Show battery-wear and lifetime estimates with their assumptions and uncertainty. Do not present a precise remaining-life claim unless the device-specific evidence supports it.
- Preserve existing Home Assistant entity IDs, cumulative energy history, and the ability to restore the previous integration/configuration during each rollout.
- Design setup, reconfiguration, sensor selection, dashboards, and alerts around ordinary household tasks. Use sensible defaults, clear progress and connection status, actionable recovery guidance, and a simple explanation of any automated charging decision; keep optional commissioning details out of the normal path.

## Project completion criteria

The integrated system is ready for routine smart charging when:

1. Growatt battery/inverter, EV, Peblar, Zonneplan, PV production forecast, and P1 inputs have documented, tested contracts with freshness and unavailable handling.
2. A nontechnical household member can complete the documented setup, an ordinary configuration change, and the basic pause/override task without editing files or asking a developer; status and errors explain the next useful action.
3. Historical electricity prices are viewable over useful time ranges with labeled units, a clear actual/forecast distinction, and averages/trend lines that respect source resolution and time changes.
4. The Zoe predictor produces a next-day SoC/charging trajectory and likely finish time with measured error and uncertainty validated on held-out real charge sessions.
5. Battery dispatch maximizes estimated net value after conversion losses and battery wear across tested dynamic-price cases, observes reserve/device/site limits, and reports assumptions and lifetime uncertainty.
6. The register map and Growatt runtime have a reviewed parity/synchronization check, and Growatt setup supports the intended network-to-serial transition while preserving identity.
7. The EMS produces correct, explainable plans from the live providers over normal, sleep/offline, missing-price, missing/stale-PV-forecast, forecast-error, and interval-boundary cases.
8. Shadow observations show the plan respects site limits, battery reserve, EV goals, device limits, and user policy.
9. Any control adapters are bounded, disabled by default, independently validated, observable, and recoverable by a human operator.
10. The RPi rollout has a fresh verified backup, recorded baseline, staged candidate, smoke checks, post-deploy observation, and tested rollback path.

Completing this roadmap does not imply Home Assistant Core acceptance for any custom integration or third-party provider.
